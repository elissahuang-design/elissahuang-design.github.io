Why I make things

My kids recently made two dolls. They colored the fabric with acrylic markers, I helped cut it out, and we stuck the pieces together with iron-on tape. One has uneven eyes. The other is a cat wearing a crown. They sleep with them every night. Before that, they made a book about a unicorn's party. The plot doesn't quite hold together, and they ask me to read it again and again.

Watching them reminded me of how I drew as a kid: hours with a pencil, trying to get what was in my head onto paper. As an adult I rarely feel that. There's always pressure to spend time on something productive, whether or not it brings any joy.

I think about this a lot as a parent right now. AI could translate every Chinese book for my kids. But reading it themselves, and stepping into the culture on their own, is something a translation can't give them. I want them to grow up using these tools to make things, not just to consume them.

I try to work that way too. When I built their reading app, AI made the visuals quickly, and I'm not attached to them. What I'm proud of is the part I spent my time on: how the lessons are structured, and why it teaches from books we already own. That's the kind of tool I like building: one that handles the polish, so people can spend their time on the part that's theirs.

Trading card grading before shipment

Trading card collectors often send cards to an independent company like PSA to be graded. An expert scores the card's condition from 1 to 10 and seals it in a tamper-proof plastic case, and a high grade can make a card worth far more. Buyers who wanted a graded card used to buy it, wait for it to arrive, then mail it to PSA themselves. I led the design of the add-on that sends it straight to PSA instead. There was no consistent pattern for services like this, and the framework meant to fix that was still being planned. I adapted the common checkbox so our work wouldn't conflict with that framework, and architected an enrollment modal that could grow. When the eBay Vault option launched later, it slotted right in.

The hardest part wasn't any one screen. It was mapping every touchpoint and every state a graded order can land in, across pages owned by different teams, and aligning those teams on one experience. Along the way I spotted a layout problem outside my scope, made it visible with a few concepts, and the owning team made it a priority. Grading shipped with adoption far above a typical add-on and strong incremental revenue. It also became a foundation: several later projects were built on its expansion, including value-added services in the cart.

AI assistant for project briefs

Design work comes with a lot of writing that isn't design. There's usually a PRD already, but I'd still be asked for a design brief or project summary: the same information, shorter, in one place. Meanwhile, the decisions that actually shaped a project were scattered across Slack threads, Zoom calls, email and docs, each with different people in them.

When eBay connected Claude to those internal tools, I built a set of Claude skills that do the gathering for me. They pull a project's context from Slack, Zoom, Outlook and Google Drive, turn it into a brief, and track which decisions are made and which are still open. They also look for adjacent projects, so conflicts and dependencies show up at kickoff instead of halfway through. The first skill took a few hours, most of it spent refining the format.

It became my project assistant. In a beta with two other designers, both called it extremely time-saving and insightful. I also packaged a toolkit of design skills that let an AI assistant work in Figma alongside you, running accessibility audits and preflight checks and updating mocks, plus Figma plugins now used by designers beyond my team, including Payments.

Price Guide for trading cards

Trading card collectors want to know what a card is worth before they buy, sell, or just to keep track of their collection. There's no sticker price. Value depends on recent sales, and it shifts with the card's condition and version. So most collectors piece it together by hand, scrolling through past sales on eBay or using other sites built on eBay's own data.

It started with a workshop that surfaced the core features collectors wanted. A price guide was one of them, alongside other tools they'd find useful, and the findings became the roadmap. I architected the foundation of Price Guide, including how it would scale beyond a single page, and led the features built on it: leading with the median price so one odd sale can't skew the value, and comparing a card across conditions and across parallels. Parallels are versions of the same card printed with a different color or finish, often in limited numbers, and a rare one can sell for many times the regular version.

Price Guide now scales across several touchpoints, from card scanning and the item page to My Collection, and has one of the highest customer satisfaction ratings. Collectors single out the comparisons as what they value most.

Chinese reading app for kids

My kids speak Chinese but couldn't read it. The apps I tried taught the language from scratch, needed reading skills just to navigate, and followed fixed lesson plans, like teaching all the numbers first. So I tested a simple method on paper (this is, where is, what is this), then architected and built an app that teaches characters backward from the books we already own, in an order that turns them into real sentences quickly.

One of my kids has learned about 50 characters and started reading a real book, and my three-year-old opens lessons alone. It's two kids in one family, not a study, but it's the best feedback I've had on anything I've designed.

Ad-free backgammon game

I heard about backgammon on a podcast and wanted to learn. Every app I downloaded was packed with ads, so I made my own. One thing led to another.

I took an existing bot engine and changed it to support different difficulty levels, then added different ways to play and several board designs. It was my first time using a Flutter game engine, and AI helped me through most of it.

Nobody asked for it. It's what happens when I get curious and have the tools to follow through.
